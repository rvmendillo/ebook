Sunlight looks white, but it is a mix of every colour of the rainbow. On its way through the atmosphere it bumps into nitrogen and oxygen molecules that are far smaller than the wavelength of light — and that is where the blue comes from.

<!-- more -->

## Rayleigh scattering

Small particles scatter short wavelengths much more strongly than long ones. The amount of scattering is proportional to one over the wavelength to the fourth power:

$$
I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4}
$$

Blue light (about 450 nm) versus red light (about 700 nm):

$$
\left(\frac{700}{450}\right)^4 \approx 5.9
$$

So blue light is scattered roughly six times more than red. Wherever you look in the sky, scattered blue light is coming towards you.

## Then why isn't the sky violet?

Violet light has an even shorter wavelength, but:

- the Sun emits less violet than blue,
- some violet is absorbed high in the atmosphere, and
- our eyes are much more sensitive to blue.

## Red sunsets

At sunset, light travels through far more air to reach you. Most of the blue is scattered away along the path, leaving the oranges and reds.

> [!NOTE]
> On Mars the effect is reversed: fine dust makes the daytime sky butterscotch and sunsets bluish.
