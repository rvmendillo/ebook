In the 1880s Hermann Ebbinghaus showed that we forget new information on a predictable curve — fast at first, then more slowly. Each time you successfully recall something, the curve flattens and you remember it for longer.

<!-- more -->

## The idea

Instead of cramming a word ten times today, review it a few times **spread out**, each gap longer than the last.

| Review | When |
|---|---|
| 1 | Same day |
| 2 | 1 day later |
| 3 | 3 days later |
| 4 | 1 week later |
| 5 | 2 weeks later |
| 6 | 1 month later |

## The Leitner box (no app needed)

1. Write each word on a card and put new cards in **Box 1**.
2. Review Box 1 daily, Box 2 every 3 days, Box 3 weekly.
3. Remember the word? Move the card **up** a box.
4. Forget it? It goes **back to Box 1**.

> [!TIP]
> Put the word in a sentence on the back of the card. Words learned in context stick far better than isolated translations.

Ten minutes a day of spaced review beats an hour of cramming once a week.
