Sheet music looks intimidating, but it answers just two questions: **which note** (up and down) and **how long** (left to right). Here is the melody you will be able to read by the end of this lesson:

```abc
X:1
T:Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
C C G G | A A G2 | F F E E | D D C2 |]
```

<!-- more -->

## 1. The staff and the treble clef

Music is written on a **staff** of five lines. The swirly **treble clef** at the start wraps around the second line from the bottom, marking it as the note **G**.

- Notes on the **lines**, bottom to top: **E G B D F** — "Every Good Boy Does Fine".
- Notes in the **spaces**, bottom to top: **F A C E** — it spells *face*.

Middle C sits on its own short line (a *ledger line*) just below the staff.

## 2. Rhythm: how long each note lasts

In the most common time signature, **4/4**, each bar holds four beats:

| Note | Beats in 4/4 |
|---|---|
| Whole note (hollow, no stem) | 4 |
| Half note (hollow, with stem) | 2 |
| Quarter note (filled, with stem) | 1 |
| Eighth note (filled, with a flag) | ½ |

The top number of the time signature says how many beats are in a bar; the bottom number says which note gets one beat (4 = quarter note).

## 3. Read the melody

Go back to *Twinkle, Twinkle* above and read it bar by bar:

1. C C G G — four quarter notes
2. A A G — two quarters, then a **half note** held for two beats
3. F F E E
4. D D C — ending on a half note

> [!TIP]
> Clap the rhythm first, then say the note names, and only then play. Separating the two skills makes both easier.

## 4. A scale to practise

The C major scale uses only the white keys of a piano:

```abc
X:2
T:C major scale
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
C D E F | G A B c | c B A G | F E D C |]
```

Read it once a day this week and note-reading will start to feel automatic.
