The Pomodoro Technique, created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s, breaks work into short, focused intervals separated by breaks.

<!-- more -->

## The basic cycle

1. Pick **one** task.
2. Set a timer for **25 minutes** and work on nothing else.
3. Take a **5-minute** break — stand up, stretch, look away from screens.
4. After four rounds, take a longer **15–30 minute** break.

## The details that make it work

- **Write distractions down** instead of acting on them. Deal with the list during breaks.
- **Plan your pomodoros** at the start of the day; estimating builds a sense of how long things really take.
- **Protect the break.** Scrolling your phone is not rest.

> [!TIP]
> If 25 minutes feels too short for deep work like coding or composing, try 50/10. The principle — focused sprints with real breaks — matters more than the exact numbers.
